Bitcoin clear signing over `@hzblj/crypto-bitcoin` 0.3.0, a dependency now (with `@hzblj/nitro-crypto` the peer). `clearSignPsbt` reads a PSBT (bytes or Base64) through the chain client into `@hzblj/crypto-clear-sign`'s display model: every input with its address, value, whether that value is checked and whose coin it is, every output as an action (a payment, change, a transfer to yourself, `OP_RETURN` data, a runestone, a script with no address), the fee and fee rate (exact once signed, else estimated as the adapter does), lock times, relative locks and replaceability. Its headline is "Send 0.01 BTC to bc1q…", a consolidation, a transfer to yourself, an etching or mint, or "Sign part of a transaction" when other wallets' coins are in it or the signer's own signature leaves it open. Accounts tell the signer's coins and change from others': one address, an account's extended public key (each key the PSBT names derived here, so change cannot be faked: `bitcoin.FALSE_DERIVATION`), or a fingerprint (the PSBT's word: `bitcoin.UNVERIFIED_CHANGE`). Every signature hash type is explained (`bitcoin.SIGHASH_NONE`, `SIGHASH_SINGLE`, `SIGHASH_SINGLE_ANYONECANPAY`, `SIGHASH_ANYONECANPAY`, `UNUSUAL_SIGHASH`, with Taproot's DEFAULT read as ALL), and the review flags foreign inputs, unchecked values, unknown fees, high fees (500 sat/vB, 0.01 BTC, or 10 % of what is sent), far-future lock times, dust, duplicate outputs, address reuse, outputs with no address or of an unknown SegWit version, and bitcoin burned in `OP_RETURN`. Runestones are deciphered exactly as `ord` does, tested against its own cases: etchings, mints, edicts and pointers, with runes sent to `OP_RETURN` (`bitcoin.RUNES_BURNED`) and cenotaphs (`bitcoin.CENOTAPH`) critical. An app's indexer can say which coins hold inscriptions or runes (the `assets` option): spending one to a stranger or into the fee is `ASSET_OWNERSHIP_TRANSFER`, destroying it `bitcoin.ASSET_BURNED`, keeping it `bitcoin.ASSET_KEPT`. `clearSignMessage` reads a BIP-322 or legacy message for an address, with `HIDDEN_CHARACTERS`, `BLIND_SIGNING` for bytes and hex, and CAIP-122 sign-in messages (`intent.signIn`, `bitcoin.SIGN_IN_MISMATCH`, `EXPIRED`). Chains are CAIP-2 `bip122:` ids, the adapter's `testnet` testnet4 unless the `testnet` option says testnet3.
